@ sundubu-jjigae | Sundubu Jjigae (Soft Tofu Stew) | Korean | 10 | 20 | 2 | 3 | Pot | Crack the egg in at the very end so the yolk stays soft.
- 1 | tbsp | vegetable oil |
- 3 | oz | pork belly | thinly sliced, cut in small pieces
- 2 | | scallion | whites and greens separated, sliced
- 2 | clove | garlic | minced
- 1 | tbsp | gochugaru |
- 1 | tbsp | soy sauce |
- 1 | tsp | toasted sesame oil |
- 1.5 | cup | water |
- 4 | oz | clams | scrubbed, optional shellfish
- 16 | oz | sundubu | one tube or tub of extra-soft tofu
- 1 | | egg |
- 0.5 | tsp | salt | or to taste
1. Heat the vegetable oil in a small pot over medium heat. Cook the pork belly and scallion whites 3 min, until the fat turns translucent.
2. Add garlic, gochugaru, soy sauce and sesame oil. Stir 30 sec, until the oil turns red and smells toasty.
3. Pour in the water and bring to a boil. Add the clams and cook 3-4 min, until the shells open. Discard any that stay shut.
4. Spoon in the tofu in large chunks without breaking them up much. Simmer 5 min, until the stew bubbles hard around the edges.
5. Season with salt. Crack the egg into the center and sprinkle with scallion greens.
6. Take the pot to the table still bubbling and serve with rice. The egg cooks in the heat of the pot.

@ doenjang-jjigae | Doenjang Jjigae (Soybean Paste Stew) | Korean | 15 | 25 | 3 | 1 | Pot | Dissolve the paste through a sieve for a smooth broth with no grit.
- 3 | cup | water |
-  |  | dried anchovies | 8, heads and guts removed (they flavor the broth and are strained out)
- 1 | piece | kombu | about 3 inches square
- 3 | tbsp | doenjang |
- 1 | tsp | gochugaru |
- 6 | oz | potato | peeled, cut in 1/2-inch cubes
- 6 | oz | zucchini | half-moons
- 0.5 | | yellow onion | cut in chunks
- 4 | oz | shiitake mushrooms | sliced
- 7 | oz | firm tofu | cut in 3/4-inch cubes
- 3 | clove | garlic | minced
- 1 | | cheongyang pepper | sliced
- 1 | | scallion | sliced
1. Bring the water, anchovies and kombu to a boil in a pot. Remove the kombu after 5 min and simmer the anchovies another 5 min. Lift out the anchovies.
2. Set a small sieve in the broth and stir the doenjang through it until dissolved. Add the gochugaru.
3. Add the potato and cook 6 min, until the edges just turn tender.
4. Add the onion, zucchini and mushrooms. Simmer 5 min, until the zucchini turns slightly translucent.
5. Add the tofu, garlic and pepper. Simmer 3 min, until the tofu is hot through.
6. Top with scallion and serve with rice.

@ galbi-jjim | Galbi Jjim (Braised Short Ribs) | Korean | 30 | 120 | 4 | 0 | Pot | Parboil the ribs first; it keeps the sauce clear instead of murky.
- 1.8 | lb | short ribs | edible meat weight; buy about 3 lb bone-in, cut Korean-style or in 2-inch pieces
- 0.5 | cup | soy sauce |
- 0.25 | cup | brown sugar |
- 2 | tbsp | mirin |
- 1 | | asian pear | grated
- 0.5 | | yellow onion | grated
- 6 | clove | garlic | minced
- 1 | tbsp | ginger | grated
- 1 | tbsp | toasted sesame oil |
- 3 | cup | water |
- 2 | | carrot | cut in chunks
- 8 | oz | korean radish | cut in 1-inch chunks
- 6 | | dried shiitake mushrooms | soaked, stems removed
- 3 | oz | chestnuts | about 8, peeled
- 2 | | red dates | optional, pitted
- 2 | | scallion | sliced
1. Soak the ribs in cold water 30 min, changing the water once. Drain.
2. Cover the ribs with fresh water in a pot, boil 5 min, then drain and rinse. Scum and blood come off here.
3. Mix the soy sauce, sugar, mirin, pear, onion, garlic, ginger and sesame oil. Add to the ribs with the 3 cups of water.
4. Bring to a boil, then cover and simmer on low 1 hour, until the meat starts to loosen from the bone.
5. Add the carrot, radish, mushrooms, chestnuts and red dates. Simmer uncovered 40 min, stirring now and then, until the vegetables are tender and the sauce coats a spoon.
6. Skim the fat from the top. Serve garnished with scallion.

@ dakgalbi | Dakgalbi (Spicy Stir-Fried Chicken) | Korean | 20 | 20 | 3 | 3 | Skillet | A little water in the pan keeps the sweet potato from scorching before it softens.
- 1.5 | lb | chicken thighs | boneless, skinless, bite-size pieces
- 3 | tbsp | gochujang |
- 1 | tbsp | gochugaru |
- 2 | tbsp | soy sauce |
- 1 | tbsp | granulated sugar |
- 1 | tbsp | toasted sesame oil |
- 4 | clove | garlic | minced
- 1 | tsp | ginger | grated
- 8 | oz | sweet potato | cut in 1/2-inch half-moons
- 0.25 | | green cabbage | cut in bite-size squares
- 1 | | yellow onion | sliced
- 8 | oz | korean rice cakes | soaked if hard
-  |  | perilla leaves | 10, sliced
- 1 | tbsp | vegetable oil |
1. Stir the gochujang, gochugaru, soy sauce, sugar, sesame oil, garlic and ginger into a paste. Toss with the chicken and let it sit 15 min.
2. Heat the vegetable oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat. Add the sweet potato and a splash of water. Cover and cook 5 min.
3. Add the chicken, cabbage, onion and rice cakes. Stir-fry 10-12 min, until the chicken is cooked through and the rice cakes are soft.
4. Add a spoonful of water if the sauce sticks to the pan.
5. Fold in the perilla leaves and cook 1 min, until wilted. Serve straight from the pan.

@ samgyetang | Samgyetang (Chicken and Rice Soup) | Korean | 20 | 70 | 2 | 0 | Pot | Soak the sweet rice for an hour first; it cooks through inside the bird.
- 1.3 | lb | whole chicken | edible meat and skin weight of a small 2 lb young chicken or Cornish hen
- 0.5 | cup | glutinous rice | soaked 1 hour, drained
- 6 | clove | garlic | peeled
- 0.75 | oz | red dates | about 4
- 1.5 | oz | chestnuts | about 4, peeled
- 1 | tbsp | ginger | sliced
- 6 | cup | water |
- 2 | | scallion | sliced
- 1 | tsp | salt | plus more at the table
- 0.5 | tsp | black pepper |
1. Rinse the chicken inside and out and trim excess fat at the cavity.
2. Pack the cavity with the rice, 3 garlic cloves, 2 red dates and the chestnuts. Cross the legs and tie them so nothing escapes.
3. Place the chicken in a pot with the water, remaining garlic, red dates and ginger. Bring to a boil, skim the foam, then cover and simmer on low 50-60 min.
4. Check that the thickest part of the thigh reads 165 F (74 C) and the rice is soft.
5. Season the broth with salt and pepper. Serve each bowl with scallion on top, and let everyone add salt to taste.

@ kimchi-bokkeumbap | Kimchi Bokkeumbap (Kimchi Fried Rice) | Korean | 10 | 10 | 2 | 2 | Skillet | Day-old rice fries up with separate grains; fresh rice turns mushy.
- 1 | tbsp | vegetable oil |
- 2 | oz | bacon | chopped
- 1 | cup | kimchi | aged, chopped
- 1 | tbsp | gochujang |
- 3 | cup | white rice | cooked, cold, day-old
- 1 | tsp | soy sauce |
- 1 | tsp | toasted sesame oil |
- 2 | | scallion | sliced
- 2 | | egg |
- 1 | tsp | sesame seeds |
1. Heat the vegetable oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat. Cook the bacon 3 min, until the fat starts to render.
2. Add the kimchi and a splash of its juice. Stir-fry 3 min, until the kimchi softens and darkens at the edges.
3. Stir in the gochujang, then add the rice, breaking up clumps. Fry 3-4 min, until the rice is hot and sticks lightly to the pan.
4. Add soy sauce, sesame oil and half the scallion. Toss and divide between two plates.
5. Fry the eggs in a separate pan until the whites are set and the yolks still run. Top the rice with the eggs, remaining scallion and sesame seeds.

@ jajangmyeon | Jajangmyeon (Noodles in Black Bean Sauce) | Korean | 20 | 30 | 4 | 0 | Skillet,Pot | Fry the black bean paste in oil first; raw paste tastes bitter.
- 3 | tbsp | vegetable oil | divided
- 0.5 | cup | chunjang | Korean roasted black bean paste
- 8 | oz | pork belly | cut in 1/2-inch cubes
- 2 | | yellow onion | cut in 1/2-inch pieces
- 8 | oz | potato | peeled, 1/2-inch cubes
- 8 | oz | zucchini | 1/2-inch cubes
- 2 | tsp | granulated sugar |
- 1 | tbsp | soy sauce |
- 1.5 | cup | water |
- 1 | tbsp | cornstarch | stirred into 3 tbsp water
- 1 | lb | chinese wheat noodles | thick fresh noodles, or udon
- 1 | | cucumber | cut in matchsticks, for serving
1. Heat 2 tbsp oil in a skillet over medium heat. Fry the black bean paste 4-5 min, stirring, until it smells nutty and loosens. Scrape it into a bowl.

3. Stir in the zucchini, fried paste, sugar, soy sauce and water. Simmer 10 min, until the potato is tender.

@ kimbap | Kimbap (Seaweed Rice Rolls) | Korean | 45 | 15 | 4 | 0 | Skillet | Season the rice while it is warm; cold rice will not roll tightly.
- 3 | cup | short-grain rice | cooked, warm
- 1 | tbsp | toasted sesame oil |
- 1 | tsp | salt | divided
- 4 | | egg | beaten with a pinch of salt
- 2 | | carrot | cut in matchsticks
- 8 | oz | spinach | blanched, squeezed, tossed with 1/2 tsp sesame oil
- 4 | oz | takuan | cut in long strips
- 1 | | cucumber | seeded, cut in long strips
- 8 | oz | fish cakes | sliced in strips, pan-fried
- 10 | g | nori | 4 whole sheets, about 2.5 g each
- 1 | tbsp | vegetable oil |
1. Mix the warm rice with sesame oil and half the salt. Spread it out to cool to room temperature.
2. Heat a little vegetable oil in a skillet. Cook the beaten egg as a thin flat omelet, then slice into long strips.
3. Saute the carrot in the same pan 2 min with the remaining salt, until barely tender.
4. Lay a nori sheet shiny side down on a bamboo mat. Spread 3/4 cup rice over it, leaving 1 inch bare at the far edge.
5. Lay a strip each of egg, carrot, spinach, takuan, cucumber and fish cake across the rice near you.
6. Roll tightly, pressing as you go, and seal with a few grains of rice. Brush the roll with sesame oil.
7. Slice into 1/2-inch rounds with a wet, sharp knife, wiping the blade between cuts.

@ pajeon | Pajeon (Scallion Pancake) | Korean | 10 | 15 | 2 | 0 | Skillet | Ice-cold water makes the edges crisp instead of bready.
- 1 | cup | all-purpose flour |
- 2 | tbsp | cornstarch |
- 0.5 | tsp | salt |
- 1 | cup | water | ice-cold
- 1 | | egg |
- 8 | | scallion | cut in 3-inch lengths
- 3 | tbsp | vegetable oil | divided
- 2 | tbsp | soy sauce |
- 1 | tbsp | rice vinegar |
- 1 | tsp | gochugaru |
- 1 | tsp | sesame seeds |
1. Whisk the flour, cornstarch and salt. Add the water and egg and whisk to a thin batter, like crepe batter.
2. Heat half the oil in a skillet over medium-high heat until it shimmers.
3. Pour in half the batter and lay half the scallions across it, pressing them in lightly.
4. Cook 3-4 min, until the bottom is golden and the edges lift. Flip and cook 3 min more, pressing down for crisp edges.
5. Repeat with the rest of the batter.
6. Mix the soy sauce, vinegar, gochugaru and sesame seeds into a dipping sauce. Cut the pancakes into squares and serve hot.
